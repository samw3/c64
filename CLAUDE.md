# C64 graphics harness: agent guide

This repo lets you make Commodore 64 graphics and *see the real result*. A scene is assembled into a C64H image (16K VIC bank, VIC-II shadow registers, color RAM, and a raster routine of up to 4K). The image runs cycle-exact in headless VICE, and the captured frames come back as PNGs. README.md has the full format and protocol.

## The loop
1. **Start a scene.** `make new SCENE=<name>` copies `scenes/_template`. Or edit an existing `scenes/<name>/gfx.py` (graphics data) or `main.asm` (VIC setup and raster code).
2. **Capture.** `make capture SCENE=<name> FRAMES=<n>`. Extra flags go in `ARGS="--crop window --zoom 3"`. Use `SKIP=` and `EVERY=` to sample long animations. Add `CRT=1` for the CRT view, or run `make crt SCENE=<name>` afterwards.
3. **Look.**
   - Read the stdout summary first: READY frame, unique frames, loop period, warnings.
   - Read `out/<name>/contact_0.png` for the sequence and `out/<name>/zoom/000.png` (2×) for pixel detail.
   - With `CRT=1`, read `out/<name>/crt/000.png` to see how the image looks on a real PAL monitor (dither and color blending).
   - `out/<name>/report.json` has per-frame color histograms, changed-pixel counts and bounding boxes, and sha1s.
4. **Critique** against the checklist below, change one thing at a time, and repeat. Keep earlier runs for comparison with `ARGS="-o out/<name>/v2"`.
5. **Check the setup without running it:** `make inspect SCENE=<name>`. Check raster timing with `make probe SCENE=<name> AT="label ..."`, which shows the line and cycle where each label runs and whether it jitters. After changing `harness/` or `include/`, run `make test`, which must stay green.

Failures exit non-zero and explain themselves:
- No READY within 500 frames → exit 2, with the PC as a KickAss label and `diagnostic.png`.
- CPU JAM → exit 3.
- `HX_SignalError(n)` → exit 4.

## Contract for main.asm (KickAssembler)
- Start with `#import "harness.asm"` and `#import "gfx.asm"` (the latter is generated by `gfx.py`).
- Then `HX_Header(start, "title", 0)` (use `HX_FLAG_NTSC` for NTSC) and `HX_VicShadow(Hashtable().put($d011, $3b, ...))`.
- Data goes in `.segment ColorRam` / `.segment Bank` via `.import binary "colorram.bin"` / `"bank.bin"`. Code goes in `.segment Code`, which starts at `$8000`, has a 4K limit, and the entry point is first.
- The init sequence is `HX_Init()`, then `HX_StartIrq(handler, line)`, then **`HX_SignalReady()`**. Capture begins with the first full frame after READY.
- In IRQ handlers, use `HX_IrqEnter()`, then your work, then `HX_NextIrq(next, line)`, then `HX_IrqExit()`.
- `HX_FrameTick()` once per frame is optional, but it makes `hx_frame` in the report meaningful for animation.
- **Runtime state:** KERNAL and BASIC are banked out. Zero page `$02-$FF` is free, as are `$0200-$3AFF` and `$9000-$CFFF`. The IRQ vector is at `$FFFE`.

## Coordinates (PAL, normal borders)
- Frames are 384×272. The display window's top-left is at **(32, 35)**, so window pixel (wx, wy) is at frame (wx+32, wy+35).
- Frame row y shows raster line **16 + y**. The display window covers raster lines 51–250.
- Sprites: X=24, Y=50 puts a sprite at the window's top-left. Its first line is drawn on raster line Y+1.
- Multicolor bitmap x (0–159) covers frame columns 32 + 2x and 33 + 2x.

## Raster timing (include/stable.asm)
Plain raster IRQs start 0–7 cycles late, so a mid-line color change wobbles. When an effect needs exact positions (raster bars, mid-line splits, FLI, opening the side borders), start the handler with one of these instead of `HX_IrqEnter`:

| Macro | Lands on | Cost | Requirements |
|---|---|---|---|
| `HX_StableDoubleIrq()` | Line L+2, cycle 3 | The rest of line L plus line L+1 | Lines L..L+2 free of badlines and sprite DMA; L ≠ 255 |
| `HX_StableTimerIrq()` (call `HX_StableTimerInit()` once after `HX_Init`) | Line L, cycle 47 | 31–38 cycles | Line L free of badlines and sprite DMA; L ≠ 0; CIA1 timer A is reserved |

- Cycles are 0-based and identical on PAL and NTSC.
- A color write whose last cycle is cycle c appears from frame column 8c − 95.
- Pad with `HX_Delay(n)`, which takes exactly n cycles.
- Choosing *which* technique, *where* to sync, and what to do with the cycles afterwards is part of the design. Verify the result with `make probe`.

## C64 graphics constraints
| Mode | Resolution | Colors |
|---|---|---|
| MC bitmap (`$D011=$3B $D016=$18`) | 160×200, double-wide pixels | Per 4×8 cell: `$D021` (shared) plus 3 free colors (`%01` screen high nybble, `%10` screen low nybble, `%11` color RAM) |
| Hires bitmap (`$D011=$3B $D016=$08`) | 320×200 | Per 8×8 cell: 2 colors (screen high nybble = set bits, low nybble = clear bits) |
| Text | 40×25 chars from a charset in the bank | Per char 1 color plus `$D021`. **Bank 1 has no ROM font**, so include your own charset. |
| MC text | 4×8 double-wide per char | Per char: color RAM bit 3 enables multicolor; `$D022`/`$D023` shared; color RAM color limited to 0–7 |
| Sprites | 8 hardware sprites, 24×21 hires or 12×21 MC | MC uses own color plus `$D025` (`%01`) and `$D026` (`%11`). Data is 64-byte aligned in the bank; pointers live at screen+`$3F8`. More than 8 requires raster multiplexing. |

- `harness.gfx.encode_mc_bitmap` / `encode_hires_bitmap` take a [y, x] array of color indices and report clashes. **Fix clashes in the art**: align shapes to cells and choose colors per cell.
- Helpers: `d018(screen, bitmap=...)` and `sprite_pointer(addr)`. `Bank.put` refuses overlapping regions.

## Palette
Colors and names: 0 black, 1 white, 2 red, 3 cyan, 4 purple, 5 green, 6 blue, 7 yellow, 8 orange, 9 brown, 10 light red, 11 dark grey, 12 grey, 13 light green, 14 light blue, 15 light grey.

**Luma order**, dark to bright: `0 | 6 9 | 2 11 | 4 8 | 12 14 | 5 10 | 3 15 | 7 13 | 1`. The pairs share a luma level, which makes them the smoothest dither partners.

Useful ramps:
- Greys: `0 11 12 15 1`
- Blue: `0 6 14 3 1`
- Warm: `0 9 2 8 10 7 1`

## Art critique checklist
- **Read at 1×.** `frames/000.png` is what a screen shows. Check that silhouettes and focal points read without zooming.
- **Value structure.** Check light vs. dark using the luma order. Hue changes at equal luma make weak edges, so contrast should carry the shapes.
- **Palette discipline.** Use few colors with deliberate ramps, and keep a consistent light direction. Check the histogram in `report.json` against your intent.
- **Dithering.** Dither only between luma neighbours. Check for patterns that turn noisy at 1×, or banding in gradients. **Judge dithers and color mixes in `crt/`, not in the exact frames.**
  - A checkerboard of an equal-luma pair becomes a solid new color.
  - On PAL, alternating lines of an equal-luma pair mix the same way.
  - A dither between colors of different luma stays visible as texture.
- **Cell structure.** Look for attribute clash and blocky 4×8 or 8×8 color steps; work with the grid rather than against it.
- **Raster work.** Splits should land on the intended lines. Jagged color changes at the end of a line are IRQ jitter, so stabilize them if they show.
- **Motion.**
  - Look at `changed_px`, `changed_bbox`, `unique_frames` and `loop_period`.
  - 1 px per frame steps are smooth; jumps or duplicate frames look stuttery.
  - A period of 2 means 25 Hz flicker.
- **Exact vs. CRT.** `frames/` and `zoom/` are pixel-exact: use them for positions, cell structure and clash. `crt/` is what a viewer sees: use it for overall color, dither blending, PAL mixes and how soft fine detail reads. The CRT model doesn't include composite/RF artifacts.

## Pitfalls
- Color RAM (`$D800`) is outside the VIC bank. The image carries it (`colorram.bin`, 1000 entries), so don't forget it.
- `$D011` bit 7 is the raster compare MSB. Set raster lines with `HX_SetRaster` / `HX_NextIrq`. Changing `$D011` mid-frame can trigger badline effects.
- `$D018` selects the screen in 1K steps and the bitmap in 8K steps (or the charset in 2K steps). Use `d018()`.
- A plain raster IRQ jitters by up to 7 cycles (it depends on the instruction it interrupts), and the captures show it. If an edge must not wobble, use a stable handler; see Raster timing.
- The testcard (`scenes/testcard`) is the reference for a working IRQ chain, sprites and color RAM.
- `scenes/afterglow` is the reference for a cycle-exact per-line kernel (`$D021`/`$D016` on every line, badlines included), a per-line-background encoder, and per-frame animation records.
